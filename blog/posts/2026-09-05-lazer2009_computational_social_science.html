Computational Social Science计算社会科学

David Lazer1, Alex Pentland2, Lada Adamic3, Sinan Aral2,4, Albert-László Barabási5, Devon Brewer6, Nicholas Christakis1, Noshir Contractor7, James Fowler8, Myron Gutmann3, Tony Jebara9, Gary King1, Michael Macy10, Deb Roy2, Marshall Van Alstyne2,11
戴维·拉泽、亚历克斯·彭特兰、拉达·阿达密克、西南·阿拉尔、阿尔贝特-拉斯洛·巴拉巴西、 德文·布鲁尔、尼古拉斯·克里斯塔基斯、诺希尔·康特拉克特、詹姆斯·福勒、迈伦·古特曼、 托尼·杰巴拉、加里·金、迈克尔·梅西、德布·罗伊、马歇尔·范·阿尔斯丁
Science 323 (5915), 721–723 · 6 February 2009 · DOI: 10.1126/science.1167742 · Published by AAAS
A field is emerging that leverages the capacity to collect and analyze data at a scale that may reveal patterns of individual and group behaviors. 一个新兴领域正在形成：它利用大规模收集与分析数据的能力，有望揭示个体与群体行为的模式。
We live life in the network. We check our e-mails regularly, make mobile phone calls from almost any location, swipe transit cards to use public transportation, and make purchases with credit cards. Our movements in public places may be captured by video cameras, and our medical records stored as digital files. We may post blog entries accessible to anyone, or maintain friendships through online social networks. Each of these transactions leaves digital traces that can be compiled into comprehensive pictures of both individual and group behavior, with the potential to transform our understanding of our lives, organizations, and societies.
我们生活在网络之中。我们定期查收电子邮件，几乎在任何地点拨打移动电话，刷交通卡乘坐公共交通，用信用卡消费。我们在公共场所的行踪可能被摄像机记录下来，病历也以数字文件的形式存储。我们可能发布任何人都能看到的博客文章，或通过在线社交网络维系友谊。这些行为中的每一项都会留下数字痕迹，而这些痕迹可以被汇编成关于个体与群体行为的全面图景，并有潜力彻底改变我们对自身生活、组织与社会的理解。
The capacity to collect and analyze massive amounts of data has transformed such fields as biology and physics. But the emergence of a data-driven "computational social science" has been much slower. Leading journals in economics, sociology, and political science show little evidence of this field. But computational social science is occurring—in Internet companies such as Google and Yahoo, and in government agencies such as the U.S. National Security Agency. Computational social science could become the exclusive domain of private companies and government agencies. Alternatively, there might emerge a privileged set of academic researchers presiding over private data from which they produce papers that cannot be critiqued or replicated. Neither scenario will serve the long-term public interest of accumulating, verifying, and disseminating knowledge. What value might a computational social science—based in an open academic environment—offer society, by enhancing understanding of individuals and collectives? What are the obstacles that prevent the emergence of a computational social science?
收集与分析海量数据的能力已经改变了生物学和物理学等领域。然而，数据驱动的"计算社会科学"的出现却要缓慢得多。经济学、社会学和政治学的顶级期刊几乎看不到这一领域的踪影。但计算社会科学确实正在发生——在谷歌（Google）、雅虎（Yahoo）等互联网公司内部，以及在美国国家安全局等政府机构之中。计算社会科学可能沦为私营公司和政府机构的专属领地；另一种可能是，出现一小群享有特权的学术研究者，他们掌握着私有数据，并据此发表无法被批评或复现的论文。这两种情形都不符合积累、验证和传播知识这一长远的公共利益。那么，一个立足于开放学术环境的计算社会科学，能够通过增进对个体与集体的理解，为社会带来什么价值？又有哪些障碍在阻碍计算社会科学的兴起？
To date, research on human interactions has relied mainly on one-time, self-reported data on relationships. New technologies, such as video surveillance (1), e-mail, and "smart" name badges, offer a moment-by-moment picture of interactions over extended periods of time, providing information about both the structure and content of relationships. For example, group interactions could be examined through e-mail data, and questions about the temporal dynamics of human communications could be addressed: Do work groups reach a stasis with little change, or do they dramatically change over time (2)? What interaction patterns predict highly productive groups and individuals? Can the diversity of news and content we receive predict our power or performance (3)?
迄今为止，关于人际互动的研究主要依赖于一次性的、自我报告的关系数据。视频监控（1）、电子邮件和"智能"名牌等新技术，能够在较长时期内提供逐时刻的互动图景，同时呈现关系的结构与内容两方面的信息。例如，可以通过电子邮件数据考察群体互动，并回答有关人类沟通的时间动态问题：工作群体是会进入一种几乎不变的停滞状态，还是会随时间发生剧烈变化（2）？什么样的互动模式预示着高产的群体与个体？我们所接收的新闻与内容的多样性，能否预测我们的权力或表现（3）？
Face-to-face group interactions could be assessed over time with "sociometers." Such electronic devices could be worn to capture physical proximity, location, movement, and other facets of individual behavior and collective interactions. The data could raise interesting questions about, for example, patterns of proximity and communication within an organization, and flow patterns associated with high individual and group performance (4).
面对面的群体互动则可以借助"社会计量仪"（sociometers）进行跨时间的评估。这类电子设备可以佩戴在身上，用以捕捉身体接近程度、位置、移动以及个体行为与集体互动的其他侧面。这些数据可以引出有趣的问题，例如：组织内部的接近与沟通模式，以及与高个体绩效和高群体绩效相关联的流动模式（4）。
We can also learn what a "macro" social network of society looks like (5), and how it evolves over time. Phone companies have records of call patterns among their customers extending over multiple years, and e-Commerce portals such as Google and Yahoo collect instant messaging data on global communication. Do these data paint a comprehensive picture of societal-level communication patterns? In what ways do these interactions affect economic productivity or public health? It is also increasingly easy to track the movements of people (6). Mobile phones allow the large-scale tracing of people's movements and physical proximities over time (7). Such data may provide useful epidemiological insights: How might a pathogen, such as influenza, driven by physical proximity, spread through a population?
我们还可以了解整个社会的"宏观"社会网络是什么样子的（5），以及它如何随时间演化。电话公司掌握着跨越多年、覆盖其全部客户的通话模式记录；谷歌、雅虎等电子商务门户则收集着全球范围的即时通讯数据。这些数据能否描绘出一幅社会层面沟通模式的全景图？这些互动又以何种方式影响经济生产率或公共健康？追踪人们的移动也变得越来越容易（6）。移动电话使得大规模、跨时间地追踪人们的移动轨迹与身体接近程度成为可能（7）。这类数据可能带来有价值的流行病学洞见：像流感这样依靠身体接触传播的病原体，会如何经由人群的接近而在人群中扩散？
The Internet offers an entirely different channel for understanding what people are saying, and how they are connecting (8). Consider, for example, this past political season, tracing the spread of arguments, rumors, or positions about political and other issues in the blogosphere (9), as well as the behavior of individuals "surfing" the Internet (10), where the concerns of an electorate become visible in the searches they conduct. Virtual worlds, which by their nature capture a complete record of individual behavior, offer ample opportunities for research—experimentation that would otherwise be impossible or unacceptable (11). Similarly, social network Web sites offer a unique opportunity to understand the impact of a person's position in the network on everything from their tastes to their moods to their health (12), whereas Natural Language Processing offers increased capacity to organize and analyze the vast amounts of text from the Internet and other sources (13).
互联网为理解人们在说什么、彼此如何联结，提供了一条完全不同的渠道（8）。例如，在刚刚过去的政治季中，可以追踪有关政治及其他议题的论点、谣言或立场在博客圈中的传播（9），也可以研究个体"冲浪"互联网的行为（10）——选民的关切就体现在他们进行的搜索之中。虚拟世界天然地记录着个体行为的完整档案，为研究提供了充分的机会——包括那些在其他情境下不可能或不被接受的实验（11）。同样，社交网站提供了一个独特的机会，让我们理解一个人在网络中的位置如何影响其方方面面——从品味、情绪到健康（12）；而自然语言处理则提升了组织与分析来自互联网及其他来源的海量文本的能力（13）。
In short, a computational social science is emerging that leverages the capacity to collect and analyze data with an unprecedented breadth and depth and scale. Substantial barriers, however, might limit progress. Existing ways of conceiving human behavior were developed without access to terabytes of data describing minute-by-minute interactions and locations of entire populations of individuals. For example, what does existing sociological network theory, built mostly on a foundation of one-time "snapshot" data, typically with only dozens of people, tell us about massively longitudinal data sets of millions of people, including location, financial transactions, and communications? These vast, emerging data sets on how people interact surely offer qualitatively new perspectives on collective human behavior, but our current paradigms may not be receptive.
简言之，一个利用前所未有的广度、深度与规模来收集和分析数据的计算社会科学正在兴起。然而，重大的障碍可能限制其进展。现有关于人类行为的概念化方式，是在无法获得太字节（TB）级数据的条件下发展起来的——这类数据描述的是整个人群中每个个体逐分钟的互动与位置。举例来说，现有的社会学网络理论大多建立在一次性"快照"数据（通常只涉及几十个人）的基础之上，面对包含位置、金融交易和通讯信息、覆盖数百万人的大规模纵向数据集，它又能告诉我们什么？这些关于人们如何互动的海量新兴数据集，无疑为理解人类集体行为提供了质性上全新的视角，但我们当前的范式可能还无法接纳它们。
There are also enormous institutional obstacles to advancing a computational social science. In terms of approach, the subjects of inquiry in physics and biology present different challenges to observation and intervention. Quarks and cells neither mind when we discover their secrets nor protest if we alter their environments during the discovery process. As for infrastructure, the leap from social science to a computational social science is larger than from biology to a computational biology, largely due to the requirements of distributed monitoring, permission seeking, and encryption. There are fewer resources available in the social sciences, and even the physical (and administrative) distance between social science departments and engineering or computer science departments tends to be greater than for the other sciences.
推进计算社会科学还面临着巨大的体制性障碍。在研究方式上，物理学和生物学的研究对象对观测与干预提出了完全不同的挑战：夸克和细胞既不会介意我们发现它们的秘密，也不会抗议我们在探索过程中改变它们的环境。在基础设施方面，从社会科学迈向计算社会科学的跨越，比从生物学迈向计算生物学的跨越更大——这主要源于分布式监测、获取许可以及加密等方面的要求。社会科学可用的资源更少；而且，与其他学科相比，社会科学院系与工程或计算机院系之间在物理空间（乃至行政上）的距离往往也更大。
Perhaps the thorniest challenges exist on the data side, with respect to access and privacy. Much of these data are proprietary (e.g., mobile phone and financial transactional information). The debacle following AOL's public release of "anonymized" search records of many of its customers highlights the potential risk to individuals and corporations in the sharing of personal data by private companies (14). Robust models of collaboration and data sharing between industry and academia are needed to facilitate research and safeguard consumer privacy and provide liability protection for corporations. More generally, properly managing privacy issues is essential. As the recent U.S. National Research Council's report on geographical information system data highlights, it is often possible to pull individual profiles out of even carefully anonymized data (15). Last year, the U.S. National Institutes of Health and the Wellcome Trust abruptly removed a number of genetic databases from online access (16). These databases were seemingly anonymized, simply reporting the aggregate frequency of particular genetic markers. However, research revealed the potential for de-anonymization, based on the statistical power of the sheer quantity of data collected from each individual in the database (17).
最棘手的挑战或许出在数据一端，涉及数据获取与隐私。这类数据大多为私有财产（例如移动电话和金融交易信息）。美国在线（AOL）公开发布其众多客户"匿名化"搜索记录后引发的惨败，凸显了私营公司共享个人数据给个人与企业带来的潜在风险（14）。要促进研究、保护消费者隐私并为公司提供责任豁免，就需要建立稳健的产学合作与数据共享模式。更一般地说，妥善管理隐私问题至关重要。正如美国国家研究理事会最近关于地理信息系统数据的报告所强调的，即便经过仔细匿名化处理的数据，也往往仍有可能还原出个人画像（15）。去年，美国国立卫生研究院（NIH）与惠康基金会（Wellcome Trust）突然将多个基因数据库从公开网络中撤下（16）。这些数据库看似已经匿名化，只是报告特定遗传标记的汇总频率；然而研究表明，凭借数据库中从每个个体收集的海量数据所蕴含的统计效力，存在去匿名化的可能（17）。
Because a single dramatic incident involving a breach of privacy could produce rules and statutes that stifle the nascent field of computational social science, a self-regulatory regime of procedures, technologies, and rules is needed that reduces this risk but preserves research potential. As a cornerstone of such a self-regulatory regime, U.S. Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) must increase their technical knowledge to understand the potential for intrusion and individual harm because new possibilities do not fit their current paradigms for harm. Many IRBs would be poorly equipped to evaluate the possibility that complex data could be de-anonymized. Further, it may be necessary for IRBs to oversee the creation of a secure, centralized data infrastructure. Currently, existing data sets are scattered among many groups, with uneven skills and understanding of data security and widely varying protocols. Researchers themselves must develop technologies that protect privacy while preserving data essential for research. These systems, in turn, may prove useful for industry in managing customer privacy and data security (18).
由于单单一起引人注目的隐私泄露事件，就可能催生出扼杀计算社会科学这一新兴领域的法规条文，因此需要建立一套由程序、技术与规则构成的自律机制，既降低这种风险，又保住研究潜力。作为这种自律机制的基石，美国的机构审查委员会（IRB）必须提升其技术知识，以理解侵害与个体伤害的可能性——因为新的可能性并不符合它们现有的伤害评估范式。许多 IRB 在评估复杂数据被去匿名化的可能性方面将会力不从心。此外，可能还有必要由 IRB 来监督建设一个安全的、集中化的数据基础设施。目前，现有数据集分散在众多团体手中，各团体在数据安全方面的技能与理解参差不齐，协议也五花八门。研究者自己也必须开发出既保护隐私、又保留研究所需关键数据的技术。这些系统反过来也可能对业界管理客户隐私与数据安全有所助益（18）。
Finally, the emergence of a computational social science shares with other nascent interdisciplinary fields (e.g., sustainability science) the need to develop a paradigm for training new scholars. Tenure committees and editorial boards need to understand and reward the effort to publish across disciplines. Initially, computational social science needs to be the work of teams of social and computer scientists. In the long run, the question will be whether academia should nurture computational social scientists, or teams of computationally literate social scientists and socially literate computer scientists. The emergence of cognitive science offers a powerful model for the development of a computational social science. Cognitive science has involved fields ranging from neurobiology to philosophy to computer science. It has attracted the investment of substantial resources to create a common field, and created enormous progress for public good in the last generation. We would argue that a computational social science has a similar potential, and is worthy of similar investments.
最后，计算社会科学的兴起与其他新兴交叉学科（如可持续性科学）一样，需要发展出一套培养新一代学者的范式。终身教职评审委员会和期刊编委会需要理解并奖励跨学科发表的努力。在初期，计算社会科学需要由社会科学家与计算机科学家组成的团队来共同完成。从长远看，问题在于：学术界应当培养"计算社会科学家"，还是应当培养由"懂计算的社会科学家"与"懂社会的计算机科学家"组成的团队？认知科学的兴起为计算社会科学的发展提供了一个有力的范本。认知科学涵盖了从神经生物学到哲学再到计算机科学的诸多领域；它吸引了大量资源投入以创建一个共同的学科领域，并在过去一代人的时间里为公共利益创造了巨大的进步。我们认为，计算社会科学具有相似的潜力，也值得相似的投入。
